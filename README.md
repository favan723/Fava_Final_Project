This project is an analysis of the dataset "Death rates for suicide, by sex, race, Hispanic origin, and age: United States". The questions I will be attempting to answer are as follows:

1. Has the age-adjusted suicide rate in the United States changed significantly over time from 1980 to 2021?
2. Is there a significant difference in suicide rates between males and females in recent years?

This repository includes an analysis of the dataset within the .qmd file attached, the dataset itself as a .csv file, a 5-minute video presentation, and a two-paragraph summary of the findings within the data written below:

Summary:
This report is meant to analyze suicide rates in the United States using data from 1950 to 2021 and answer the questions: "Has the age-adjusted suicide rate in the United States changed significantly over time from 1980 to 2021?" and "Is there a significant difference in suicide rates between males and females in recent years?" As for the first question, we start by filtering out data points from years before 1980. As seen in the .csv file, before 1980, the columns are separated by decade intervals as opposed to year intervals. Then, running a linear regression test shows a p-value of 8.338e-05, suggesting that there has been a significant change in suicide rates in the US from 1980 to 2021. We can then analyze the line graph of suicide rates to see that the rate significantly dipped from the late 90s to the early 2000s, and has been on a large rise since 2005.

Getting to the second question, we exclude the years of data before 2015 this time, filter out the data that does not take sex into account, and summarize the remaining data. Looking at the summary, the suicide rate mean for males sits comfortably higher than the rate for females, at 22.27 and 6,13, respectively. Following this with a two-sample t-test, we can confirm the results with a 95% confidence interval that the difference between male and female rates lies between -17.46 and -14.83. And continuing with the t-test, we see a p-value of 3.12e-81, again confirming that there is a significant difference in the rates across sex. Finally, plotting both a boxplot and histogram confirms the findings, with males again having a significantly higher rate of suicide than females across the years 2015 to 2021.
